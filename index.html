How it works

  • Each row is one continuous copper strip; Cut breaks it between holes.
  • Click two holes for a wire; click once for components.
  • Right-click or R rotates the next component.
  • In Select mode: click a component to inspect, drag to move, hover a pin for its name.
  • Use Erase to remove components or cuts.

Welcome to Stripboard Circuit Builder

Design real stripboard circuits visually — place components, draw wires, cut tracks, and wire up a battery. Save your work, load it back, and iterate. Each section below is collapsible; open any of them to learn more.

📌 Quick start
  1. Click a component in the toolbar (Resistor, LED, NPN…)
  2. Click any hole on the board to drop it — your click becomes the component's centre
  3. Switch to the Wire tool, pick a colour, then click two holes to connect them
  4. Click the battery's red or black terminal, then click a hole to attach that clip
  5. Hit Save to download your circuit as a JSON file; use Load to bring one back
🟫 The stripboard
  • Each horizontal row is one continuous copper strip — components sharing a row are electrically joined
  • Use the Cut tool to break a strip between two adjacent holes (shown as a small ×). Click again to remove the cut.
  • Wires can bridge any two holes on the board, including across multiple rows
  • Change board size with the Cols × Rows inputs, then click Resize

🔌 Wires
  • Pick a colour from the top swatches or the custom colour picker, then click two holes
  • Select a wire and the endpoints become draggable blue handles — drag either one to reshape
  • With a wire selected, clicking any colour swatch recolours that wire instantly (no need to delete and redraw)
  • Drag the middle of the wire instead to move both endpoints together
🔋 Battery & inline switch
  • The battery sits off-board on the left. Click its red (+) or black (−) terminal — the next click on a hole places that clip
  • Click the battery body to select it. Edit Voltage in the properties panel (0.5 V – 48 V)
  • Tick Switch on + wire to add an inline switch on the red lead — see the picture above
  • Click the switch directly to toggle ON / OFF — works regardless of the active tool
  • Use the Remove + clip / Remove − clip buttons in the panel to unplace a clip
🎯 Select, move, rotate, mirror
  • Pick Select (or press V), then click a component to highlight it
  • Drag to reposition. Release outside the board and the move is reverted automatically
  • R rotates the selected component around its centre — the centre pin for 3-pin parts, the body centre for ICs
  • M mirrors the selected component (flips its pin order)
  • Delete / Backspace removes the selection
  • Esc deselects and returns to Select mode
  • Hover any pin to see its name in a tooltip ("Collector — Q1", etc.)
  • The toolbar's Rotate button rotates the selected component (or, if nothing is selected, the placement angle for the next part you drop)
⚙️ Properties panel
  • Label — each component gets an auto-assigned designator (R1, C1, Q1, U1…) you can rename
  • Value — for resistors, type 4.7k, 470, 1M and the body's colour bands update live
  • Span — resistors can have their leads further apart (2–10 holes)
  • Wiper — drag the slider on a variable resistor to set the screw position
  • Model — diodes (e.g. 1N4148), NPNs (e.g. BC547) and the 555 show an editable model name on the body
  • Colour — LEDs and wires each have their own colour picker in the panel
  • Opacity — fade a component to see what's underneath it
  • The Rotate / Mirror / Delete buttons at the bottom always act on the current selection
✂️ Cuts & the Erase tool
  • Cut tool: click between two adjacent holes on a row to break the copper strip there
  • Cuts are reversible — click the same spot again to remove the cut
  • Erase tool: click a component to remove it, or click an existing cut to clear it
  • For sensitive deletes use Select → Delete so you can Ctrl+Z if you change your mind
💾 Save · Load · Undo · Redo · Clear
  • Save downloads your circuit as a small JSON file (everything is preserved: components, cuts, battery, switch state, labels)
  • Load opens any saved circuit — yours or someone else's
  • Ctrl+Z undoes the last action · Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) redoes
  • Clear wipes the board — it asks for confirmation first
🔍 Zoom & viewport
  • Scroll wheel over the board zooms in / out, anchored on the cursor (30 % – 500 %)
  • Ctrl+0 resets zoom to 100 %
  • The board's scrollbars pan when zoomed in beyond the viewport
